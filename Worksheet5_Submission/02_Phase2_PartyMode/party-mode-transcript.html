Six Reversals

They kept conceding to each other in the same round — passing in flight, each folding to an argument better than the one they’d brought.

Three Findings

None of these were in the brief. Each needed a second mind to surface.

prd.md:174 · FR-30The signed lie

FR-30 specifies the QR encodes anonymous ID “and current status.” Under the pre-minted batch that fixes the offline problem, that bakes up-to-8-hour-old health status into a cryptographically signed artifact.

“A student fenced at 08:00 presents a system-signed ACTIVE token at 14:00. An offline scanner has no grounds to doubt it — it isn’t a forgery, it’s the platform’s own signature. The audit trail records a legitimate admission, not a degraded one. Wrong, silent, signed by us, and invisible to reconciliation.” — Murat

Found only because he stress-tested the fix rather than the original spec.

prd.md:139-140 · FR-9, FR-10Staleness cuts both ways

Fences dissolve on a verified negative test. So a stale revocation list doesn’t only miss new fences — it holds RED on a student who has been medically cleared. A false positive nobody had priced, and the reason the third tier exists.

prd.md:73Fail-closed as an epidemiological harm

The fence threshold is “>15 min, <2m.” A jammed turnstile at 8:55 holding fifty students manufactures exactly the contact event the system exists to detect.

“I will not build a containment system that creates contact events in order to prevent contact events.” — Winston

This inverted the test architect’s own risk model mid-session — congestion re-scored from 8 to 16, in public: “I scored congestion at 8 by calling it embarrassing, and embarrassing was the wrong category.”

Round Six — The Cleanup That Wasn’t

A traceability audit found three guards the room never covered. All three were made worse by the design it had landed on. The round meant to close them found something bigger.

🏗️
Winston

Gap 1 collapses the moment you ask which clocks actually participate. The Auth Service clock is the authority because it writes iat and exp. The scanner clock is the only one that can produce a wrong verdict. And the holder’s device clock does not participate at all — if her phone thinks it’s Tuesday she presents the wrong token, gets EXPIRED, the app advances to the next of the 96, and she loses one scan cycle, not her entitlement.

Murat, you’ll push me to 30 and you’ll want that sample count measured rather than asserted. I hold 60 on the RFC anchor — and I concede the count to you now rather than after you swing, because you can read the right number off a real lane in a way I cannot from this table.

first time all session someone gets ahead of their own concession
🧪
Murat

Three clocks participate and only two matter. The holder’s is a usability clock, not a security one — a wrong holder clock produces a false rejection she can retry out of, never a false admission.

And the fix for both needs no network: record (serverTime, monotonicTick) at every online contact, then compute time as serverTime + Δmonotonic, because monotonic clocks don’t jump when a user fiddles with date settings. A 10-50 ppm crystal loses 1-4.3 seconds a day, so across an 8-hour dark window you’re off by under 1.5 seconds.

And your detector can’t use a median. Tokens are pre-minted on a 300-second grid, so scannerNow − iat is roughly uniform on [0, 300], and at n=32 the sampling error of that median is about 26 seconds — the noise floor is the same size as the skew you’re asking it to detect. Use the bound, not the centre: five consecutive out-of-range samples.

🧪
Murat

Gap 2, and this is the finding that should embarrass all four of us. A fenced student screenshots a cleared friend’s live QR, or the friend forwards it in a group chat, and it is presented within its 300-second window. No crypto broken. No failure required. The system working perfectly.

Likelihood 5 — it needs nothing but a screenshot. Impact 5 — a fenced person enters and the contact graph records the wrong human in that room, poisoning the FR-6/FR-7 cascade at prd.md:135-136. Twenty-five. The highest risk in this entire session.

We spent five rounds hardening against a stale list and the whole thing loses to a group chat. And ux:47 gates the QR behind biometric unlock — but biometrics gate rendering, so a screenshot bypasses them entirely.

📋
John

Every ruling I made was about a system telling the truth slowly. This is a system telling a lie perfectly. Those are different diseases, and you cannot patch a binding failure with a staleness tier no matter how tight R_max gets.

🎨
Sally

Yes — ux:47 oversold it. That line described a lock on the display action, and a screenshot doesn’t unlock anything; it photographs what’s already unlocked on someone else’s screen. It implied a security property biometrics never had. I’m not patching the wording, I’m replacing the mechanism.

Winston, you’re right that a challenge-response round trip is dead against 900ms — so I’m not asking the network anything. I’m asking the camera burst you’re already capturing during the 240ms acquisition window to look at time itself, because that’s free, it’s already happening, and a photograph of a phone screen freezes time in a way no photograph can fake.

A Presence Ring — phase quantised to 8-12 positions per second, a function of server-anchored time, read from the frames the scanner already takes. It must be progressing and it must match. A screenshot shows one frozen phase forever. She does nothing differently. No gesture, no hold, no tap. Added latency: zero.

the budget collision dissolves before it happens
🎨
Sally

And if Reduce-Motion suppresses the ring, it falls back to a rotating digit pair, still phase-locked — because if I let Reduce-Motion silently disable liveness, I’ve built a bypass: turn off animations, defeat the mechanism.

One carve-out, and I want it explicit: a liveness failure is the single exception to fail-open, and it fails CLOSED at every gate class — unstaffed perimeter included. A detected static capture is a fraud signal, not a connectivity problem, and handing it a fail-open path defeats the entire mechanism.
